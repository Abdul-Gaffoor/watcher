# Elliott Wave, on one page

A working summary of the cycle, the rules that cannot be broken, and the
guidelines that usually hold.

## The rules

These three are absolute. A count that breaks one of them is not an Elliott
count, however much you would like it to be.

1. Wave 2 never retraces more than 100% of wave 1.
2. Wave 3 is never the shortest of waves 1, 3 and 5.
3. Wave 4 never enters the price territory of wave 1.

## The guidelines

> Guidelines describe what usually happens. They are how you rank competing
> counts, not how you prove one.

- **Alternation.** If wave 2 is sharp, wave 4 tends to be sideways, and the
  other way round.
- **Equality.** When wave 3 is extended, waves 1 and 5 often end up a similar
  length.
- **Channelling.** Connect the ends of 2 and 4, then draw a parallel from the
  end of 1 — wave 5 often finishes near it.

## Common retracements

| Wave | Typical retracement |
| ---- | ------------------- |
| 2    | 50% – 61.8% of 1    |
| 4    | 38.2% of 3          |
| B    | 50% – 78.6% of A    |

## Working a count

```text
1. Find the degree you are trading, then label one degree above and below.
2. Mark the three rules against every candidate count.
3. Discard the ones that break a rule. Rank the rest by the guidelines.
4. Write down what would invalidate the count. Trade that level, not the count.
```

The last step is the one people skip. A count without an invalidation level is
a story, not a plan.
